Bolivia is divided into 9 departments (similar to states or provinces). Each has its own capital, geography, and cultural identity. Here’s a clear overview:

1. La Paz

Capital: La Paz (seat of government)

Geography: Andes mountains, Altiplano, Lake Titicaca

Notable for: Political center, Indigenous Aymara culture, dramatic terrain

Economy: Government, commerce, tourism

2. Santa Cruz

Capital: Santa Cruz de la Sierra

Geography: Lowland plains and tropical forests

Notable for: Economic powerhouse, fastest population growth

Economy: Agriculture (soy, sugar), industry, energy

Culture: More Caribbean-influenced, business-oriented

3. Cochabamba

Capital: Cochabamba

Geography: Valleys with mild climate

Notable for: Known as Bolivia’s “breadbasket”

Economy: Agriculture, services

Culture: Famous for food and festivals

4. Potosí

Capital: Potosí

Geography: High Andes

Notable for: Cerro Rico silver mine, key to colonial history

Economy: Mining

UNESCO: Historic city of Potosí

5. Oruro

Capital: Oruro

Geography: Altiplano

Notable for: Carnival of Oruro (UNESCO Intangible Heritage)

Economy: Mining, folklore tourism

6. Chuquisaca

Capital: Sucre (constitutional capital of Bolivia)

Geography: Valleys and mountains

Notable for: Colonial architecture, birthplace of independence

Economy: Education, agriculture

Culture: Legal and historical center

7. Tarija

Capital: Tarija

Geography: Southern valleys

Notable for: Wine and singani production

Economy: Natural gas, viticulture

Culture: Relaxed lifestyle, strong regional identity

8. Beni

Capital: Trinidad

Geography: Amazonian plains, rivers, wetlands

Notable for: Low population density, biodiversity

Economy: Cattle ranching, forestry

Culture: Indigenous Amazonian traditions

9. Pando

Capital: Cobija

Geography: Amazon rainforest

Notable for: Smallest population, remote

Economy: Brazil nut harvesting, forestry

Culture: Strong cross-border influence from Brazil
